Hi everyone, Reporting a regularity in how the Task 2 queue_onset windows appear to be selected. It does not expose any label and needs no data after T, but it makes onset windows much easier than intended, so I think everyone should know about it and you should decide whether it is acceptable.

What I see

For the train split, queue labels can be derived from train/mainline_states (speed <= 0.6 x free-flow speed). Doing that for the 40 shipped queue_onset train windows (8 corridors x 5):

In 39 of 40 windows the first queued cell in the horizon appears at the last step, T+30. In no window does a queue appear at T+5 … T+25. (The remaining window has no queued cell among the cells with an observed speed.) The windows are small: median 3 queued cells, all at T+30. This is what you would get if the selector scans origins forward in time and keeps the earliest origin whose 30-minute horizon contains a queue: that origin is, by construction, the one where the queue starts at the very end of the horizon.

Why it matters

If validation and private windows were selected the same way, then for a queue_onset window a participant knows, before looking at any traffic, that steps T+5 … T+25 are empty and that a queue starts at exactly T+30. The task collapses from "when and where will a queue form in the next 30 minutes" to "which few links are queued at T+30, given that one starts right then". On the shipped train onset windows, with their dates held out of training:

a general forecaster trained on windows where onset is spread over all six steps: IoU 0.57 the same features, trained only on windows where the first queue is at T+30 and predicting only that step: IoU 0.93 So roughly a third of an IoU point on half of the Task 2 windows comes from the selection rule rather than from forecasting skill. condition is published in window_index.csv, so the rule can be applied per window.

To reproduce

For each row of task2//train/window_index.csv with condition == queue_onset, take the six timestamps after forecast_origin from corridors//train/mainline_states, mark speed_kmh <= 0.6 * free_speed_kmh (from fd_parameters.csv), and record the first step with any marked link. It is step 6 every time.

Questions

Is this selection behaviour intended, and is a method that learns it from the released train windows within the rules? If it is not intended, would you consider drawing onset origins at a random offset before the first queued step (so first appearance is spread over T+5 … T+30)? For transparency: my current submission does use this, and I am happy to switch back to the general model depending on your answer. I would rather flag it now than have it surface at verification.

A participant reported a regularity in how the Task 2 windows were selected. It is real, we have checked it on all three splits, and this post hands it to everyone.

For windows marked queue_onset in window_index.csv, the timing of the answer is fixed. In all 120 of them the queue appears only at T+30, and the first five steps, T+5 through T+25, are empty. The cause is the window selector. It keeps the earliest origin whose 30-minute horizon contains a queue, and for a queue that begins at time t that origin is t minus 30 minutes, so the queue lands on the final step by construction.

queue_ongoing windows are not affected. That condition is defined by the 60 minutes of history already containing a queue, so a queue at the start of the horizon follows from the definition, and the history is published anyway.

We are leaving the data and the metric as they are. The property comes from the selection rule rather than from the values, it can be derived from the train split, which ships with its answers, and it is now public. Rebuilding the data a third time seven weeks before the deadline would cost everyone more than it is worth, and redefining S_queue to ignore time would turn Task 2 into a different task.

Using it is within the rules. You may condition on the condition column and place your predictions accordingly. Now that every team has been told, it is not an advantage held by anyone in particular.

One thing worth knowing: under a space-time IoU, spreading a prediction evenly across all six steps is expensive. What Task 2 still asks, and what will separate the leaderboard, is which links queue.

We would rather hand everyone the same information than let the ranking turn on who noticed.

Task 2 の `queue_onset` 120窓すべてについて、

$$
T+5,\ T+10,\ T+15,\ T+20,\ T+25
$$

は必ず空で、queue は

$$
\boxed{T+30}
$$

にだけ出ます。

理由は window selector です。実際のqueue開始時刻を \(t\) とすると、selector は「30分以内にqueueが入る最も早いforecast origin」を選びます。したがって選ばれるoriginは

$$
T=t-30\text{ min}
$$

となり、queue開始時刻 \(t\) は必ず forecast horizon の最後、

$$
T+30
$$

に一致します。これはデータ値から偶然見つかった相関ではなく、**window選択アルゴリズムから決定論的に生じる性質**です。

主催者はさらに、これを train / validation / private の3 split全部で確認したと言っています。つまり trainだけの癖ではありません。

したがって `queue_onset` では、予測時に

$$
\hat Y_{T+5:T+25}=0
$$

と固定してよく、実際に予測すべきなのは

$$
\boxed{\hat Y_{T+30}}
$$

の空間位置だけです。

主催者が最後に

> What Task 2 still asks ... is which links queue.

と言っているのはその意味です。`queue_onset` に関しては「いつqueueになるか」はもう競技問題ではなく、残るのは

> T+30でどのlinkがqueueになるか

だけです。

さらに重要なのが space-time IoU の話です。

仮に正解がT+30の3cellだけだとして、モデルが同じ3cellを6 horizon全部に予測するとします。

正解集合のサイズは

$$
|Y|=3
$$

ですが、予測集合は

$$
|\hat Y|=18
$$

です。intersectionはT+30の3cellだけなので、

$$
IoU=\frac{3}{18}=\frac16
$$

程度まで落ちます。

つまり「時間が分からないから6ステップ全部に薄く出しておく」は非常に高くつきます。主催者が

> spreading a prediction evenly across all six steps is expensive

と言っているのはこれです。

一方 `queue_ongoing` は影響なしです。

`ongoing` は定義上、直前60分のhistoryにすでにqueueが存在します。そのhistory自体が公開されているので、

> horizon開始時点でもqueueがある可能性が高い

という情報はもともと観測から分かります。`queue_onset` のようにselection ruleから隠れた未来時刻が確定してしまう問題ではありません。

競技ルール上も完全に使用可能と明言されています。

主催者の立場は、

* propertyはselection rule由来
* train answerから導出可能
* 今は全員に公開済み
* condition列を使ってよい
* predictionをそのconditionに応じて配置してよい

というものです。

なので実装上は、かなり強く、

```text
if condition == queue_onset:
    pred[T+5:T+25] = 0
    pred[T+30] = onset_spatial_model(...)
```

でよいです。

さらに学習側も、一般的な6-step onset modelより、

$$
X_{\le T}
\rightarrow
Y_{T+30}
$$

だけを学習するsingle-horizon modelに寄せた方が自然です。

今回のアナウンスによって、Task 2 は実質かなり明確に二分されます。

$$
\boxed{
\text{queue\_onset}
=
\text{T+30 spatial localization}
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
\text{queue\_ongoing}
=
\text{6-step spatiotemporal forecasting}
}
$$

です。

今のあなたたちのonset側で一番重要なのは、もはや時間方向ではなく、**T+30でqueueになる数リンクをどれだけ正確に選ぶか**です。これまで苦戦していた「top2-3候補のどれが本当のbottleneckか」という問題が、ほぼそのまま競技の核心になっています。
